# Venue Quality Policy

This review intentionally uses a **high-selectivity evidence policy** because its primary purpose is to identify a defensible research gap suitable for strong venues.

## Core evidence: include

Peer-reviewed full papers from recognized top conferences and high-quality journals, including when directly relevant:

- CVPR, ICCV, ECCV
- NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR
- TPAMI, IJCV
- AAAI and similarly established A/A* conferences when the work is methodologically central
- Other established Q1 journals only after venue-quality verification

## Core evidence: exclude

- arXiv-only or other preprint-only manuscripts
- withdrawn/rejected submissions
- MDPI journals
- predatory, low-ranked, or questionable venues
- papers whose only accessible version is a non-peer-reviewed manuscript
- workshop papers as evidence for novelty claims
- theses, technical reports, blog posts, patents, and vendor white papers as core scientific evidence

## Context-only sources

A workshop paper or non-core source may be recorded in a separate context table only if it is uniquely useful for tracing terminology, a dataset, or an emerging direction. It must **never be counted as primary evidence of a research gap** and must be clearly marked `context_only`.

## Publication-version rule

When a peer-reviewed version exists, cite and analyze the final published version rather than a preprint.

## Quality verification fields

Every included paper should record:

- venue
- year
- peer-reviewed status
- venue tier/rank evidence
- publication type
- DOI or publisher/proceedings URL where available
- whether a preprint exists but is not used as the canonical citation

## Principle

A smaller corpus of directly relevant, rigorously screened top-quality work is preferred over a larger but noisy corpus.
